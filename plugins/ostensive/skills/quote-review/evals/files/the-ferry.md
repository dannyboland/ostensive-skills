# The Ferry

Mara walked quickly down to the slip, her heart beating very fast, and she looked anxiously at the grey water. The ferry was late. The ferry was always late in November, her mother had said, and her mother's mother, and the women before them who had stood on this same slip in the same wind waiting for the same boat.

She was suddenly aware that she was being watched. The ticket-seller, a really quite old man who was somewhat deaf, stared at her through the smeared glass, thinking that she looked exactly like the Havel girl who had drowned, and wondering if he should say something. Mara wondered what he was staring at.

The rope had been left coiled on the bollard by someone. A decision was made. She picks it up and it is heavy and wet and it smells of tar and diesel and she thinks of her father's hands and she drops it again because the horn sounds, low, out past the point, and then the ferry came round the headland slowly, very slowly, like something that did not want to arrive.

"I'm going," she said determinedly.

"You're going," he agreed sadly, nodding his head up and down.
